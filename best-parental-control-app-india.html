Best parental control app in India (2026): an honest comparison

Most parental-control roundups score apps by how much they monitor — messages read, screenshots taken, location pinned. That measures surveillance, not protection. The question that decides whether a filter actually helps is narrower: does the blocking hold when a curious child tries to switch it off, on every device they use, on any network. This page compares Zaksha with four widely used apps on that basis, with prices in rupees.

Facts checked 7 September 2026.

Zaksha is a paid service for Indian families: it blocks whole categories on iPhone, Android, Windows and Mac, keeps filtering on when the device leaves your Wi-Fi, and messages you on WhatsApp the moment a device stops filtering. It costs ₹199/month for the household, five devices included, with a 14-day free trial and no card needed to start. It does less than some apps below on purpose, which the comparison makes plain.

What Zaksha deliberately does not do

Bark and Qustodio can read a child's messages, flag risky content, and show you where the phone is. Bark's whole design is monitoring — it scans texts, email and social feeds and alerts you to what looks dangerous. If that is what you want, they do it well and Zaksha does not do it at all. We block; we don't surveil. That is the pitch, stated plainly.

Zaksha has no per-app time limits — it schedules the internet on and off in time windows per child, not individual apps by the minute. It does no location tracking. It reads no messages and takes no screenshots. It sees the website names a device asked for and nothing on the page, keeps seven days of that, then deletes it. If you need a per-app minute budget or a live location dot, another app on this page fits better.

The reason is one trade we made on purpose: a filter a child can quietly switch off is worse than useless, because it tells a parent everything is fine when it isn't. So the effort goes into blocking that holds and an alert the moment it stops, rather than into a monitoring feed. On a family iPhone, turning filtering off locks your child's chosen apps until it is back on; the honest limit is that Apple lets the Apple ID password reset the Screen Time passcode, so that password is the real key. On Android you pick the level — the standard install alerts you if it is removed, and the fresh-phone setup is tamper-proof, meaning Zaksha can't be uninstalled from the phone.

FAQ

Common questions

Which is cheapest?

Google Family Link is free, and for an Android-only home with no laptops it may be all you need. Among the paid apps, Zaksha is a flat ₹199/month for the whole household with five devices, where most others price per device or step up sharply for the plan that includes filtering plus alerts. Compare the plan that has the features you actually want, not the headline starting price.

Which ones cover iPhone, Windows and Mac, not just Android?

Zaksha, Qustodio and Mobicip run on all four — Android, iPhone, Windows and Mac. Family Link is Android-first and does less on iPhone, with no Windows or Mac support. If your child has an Android phone and a Windows laptop for classes, the free option leaves the laptop uncovered.

I want to read my child's messages and see their location — which app?

Not Zaksha. Bark is built for that: it scans texts, email and social feeds and alerts you to risky content, and it tracks location. Qustodio and Mobicip also do location and some monitoring. Zaksha does none of it by design — it blocks categories and tells you if filtering stops, and it can't see messages, photos or location.

Can a child just uninstall it?

It depends on the setup. On a family iPhone, Zaksha is pinned with a Screen Time passcode only you know, so it can't be removed without it — though Apple lets the Apple ID password reset that passcode, so keep it private. On Android, the standard install is tamper-evident: you get a WhatsApp and push alert the moment it is removed. The Android fresh-phone setup is tamper-proof — it can't be uninstalled from the phone at all. Qustodio also has uninstall protection behind the parent password; a plain Bark install can be removed unless you use the Bark Phone hardware.
