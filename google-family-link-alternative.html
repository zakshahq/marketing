Where Family Link stops

Two gaps that show up fast.

Facts checked 7 September 2026.

iPhone support
If your child signs into their Google Account on an iPhone or iPad, Family Link's screen time limits, app blocking, app activity reports and Chrome restrictions don't work. Google's own support pages say so directly: those controls apply on Android, not iOS.
Category filtering
There's no single switch for adult content, gambling or social media. What you get is Google SafeSearch, Play Store age ratings, and a Chrome site list you build one URL at a time. Browsing outside Chrome, or outside Google's ecosystem, isn't filtered.
What Zaksha does differently

Four things Family Link doesn't cover.

Blocks whole categories, including adult content

Adult, gambling, social media, gaming, streaming and AI chatbots — each category one switch, set per child. Add your own sites to the block list.

An app on the device, not just Google's rules

A native app on iPhone and Android, a small agent on Windows, a configuration profile on Mac. Filtering travels with the device — mobile data, a friend's Wi-Fi, anywhere.

Built to stay on

Switch filtering off and you get a WhatsApp message and push alert at once. On iPhone the child's apps lock until it's back on; on Android the fresh-phone setup can't be uninstalled. The honest limit: on iPhone, your Apple ID password can reset it.

Schedules and per-child rules

Internet off at bedtime, on after homework. Time windows per day, set per child or per device, from one dashboard.

What Zaksha doesn't do

Where Family Link still wins.

Zaksha has no per-app time limits, no location tracking and no message monitoring. Family Link's screen-time limits — daily budgets, bedtime, per-app timers — are genuinely good, and free. If that's your main need on a single Android phone, keep using it. Zaksha's job is category blocking that holds across every device your child actually uses.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Should I keep Family Link or switch to Zaksha?

If your child has one Android phone and your main concern is screen time, Family Link covers that for free. Switch to Zaksha, or add it alongside, once there's an iPhone in the mix, or you want category blocking rather than a Play Store age rating.

Can I run Zaksha and Family Link at the same time?

Yes. They don't conflict — Family Link keeps handling Play Store approvals and its own screen-time timers, and Zaksha handles category blocking and the cross-device rules Family Link doesn't reach.

Can my child get around it?

On iPhone, switching filtering off locks the child's chosen apps until it's back on — the Screen Time passcode does the pinning, and only your Apple ID password can reset that, so keep it private. On Android, the standard install is tamper-resistant and alerts you the moment it's removed; the fresh-phone (device-owner) setup is tamper-proof — Zaksha can't be uninstalled from the phone at all.
