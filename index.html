FAQ

Questions, answered.

Can my child turn it off? Is Zaksha tamper-proof?

On a family iPhone a child can switch DNS filtering off — Screen Time doesn't prevent that — but Zaksha then blocks the app types you've chosen and alerts you on WhatsApp, keeping them paused for a 30-minute cooldown once it's back on; a Screen Time passcode you set stops them deleting the app. Keep your Apple ID password private, since Apple lets it reset that passcode. On Android you choose the level: the standard install is tamper-evident — you're alerted the moment it's removed — and the fresh-phone setup is tamper-proof: Zaksha can't be uninstalled. For offices and schools, our supervised (MDM) setup is tamper-proof across the fleet.

Does it work on mobile data, not just home Wi-Fi?

Yes. The Zaksha app lives on the device, so filtering follows your child onto mobile data and any Wi-Fi — not just your home network.

What exactly can Zaksha see?

Website names only — that a device asked for youtube.com, never your messages, photos, passwords or what's on a page. History is kept for a rolling 7 days, then genuinely deleted. Everything is processed and stored on our servers in India and never leaves them. No ads, and nothing is sold.

Which categories can I block?

Adult content, gambling, social media, gaming, streaming — and AI chatbots like ChatGPT and Gemini, which parents switch off in exam season. Each category is one switch, set separately for each child's device, and you can add specific sites of your own to the block list.

Does Zaksha block ads and trackers? What about YouTube ads?

Turning on Ads & trackers blocks the third-party ad and tracker networks that follow your child around the web — the pop-ups, banners and trackers across the sites and apps they use. It doesn't strip the ads shown inside an app like YouTube, because those are served from the same place as the video itself, so blocking them would stop the app working. In short: it cuts third-party advertising and tracking, not an app's own in-stream ads.

How do I set Zaksha up on each device?

iPhone, iPad and Android use the Zaksha app; Windows installs a small agent (as an administrator) and Mac uses a configuration profile. For the strongest Android setup, you set Zaksha up on a freshly reset phone as its device owner, so it can't be removed. You get guided, step-by-step instructions for each platform on the Get the app page.
