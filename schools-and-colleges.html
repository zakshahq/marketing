Built to stay on

A student can't just switch it off.

On Windows, a student account without admin rights can't stop or remove Zaksha. On supervised iPads the profile can't be removed. Switching to an encrypted DNS service doesn't get around it, and if a device stops reporting, the console flags it.

Why Zaksha

Protection, not surveillance.

Zaksha does

  • Blocks the categories and sites you choose
  • Follows class, lab and test hours
  • Keeps a 7-day record of sites looked up, per device
  • Keeps data in India

Zaksha doesn't

  • Read messages or chats
  • Record keystrokes or take screenshots
  • Need students' passwords
  • Sell or share data

Students' own phones on campus Wi-Fi?

On request, your campus or hostel network can point at a dedicated Zaksha instance, so every device on that Wi-Fi gets the same rules, phones included. It is one policy for the whole network, and a VPN or mobile data gets around it. Ask us when you enquire.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Are adult sites blocked all the time, or only in school hours?

All the time, once it's on. You choose which categories follow a schedule, such as social media, games, streaming and AI tools.

Can we turn AI tools off only during a test?

Yes. Put the lab in a group with a schedule; AI tools are paused inside the window and back after it.

Does it read what students type?

No. It sees which sites a device looks up and whether they were blocked.

What if a student changes the DNS setting?

On Windows without admin rights they can't change it. Encrypted DNS services are blocked, and a device that stops reporting shows up in the console.

We already have a firewall.

A firewall covers your network. Zaksha covers each device on any network, and keeps a record per device.

Where is our data?

In India, on AWS Mumbai. A dedicated instance is available on paid contracts.
