What changes once Zaksha is on

You decide what's reachable

Turn categories on or off: adult, gambling, social media, gaming, streaming, AI tools. Add your own sites to block or allow. Start from a ready-made policy, such as a security baseline that blocks malware and phishing.

Rules follow your timetable

Put devices in groups, such as a lab, a department or a floor, and give each group its own schedule.

It stays on

On Windows, an account without admin rights can't stop or remove Zaksha. On supervised iPads and iPhones the profile can't be removed. If a device stops reporting, the console tells you.

You can answer "what happened?"

See which sites each device looked up in the last 7 days and which were blocked, then export it.

Which devices

Windows PCs and laptops

The Zaksha agent. Staff and students without admin rights can't remove it.

iPhones, iPads and Macs

A configuration profile. It can't be removed on supervised devices.

Android phones and tablets

Private DNS. It works, but someone with access to Settings can turn it off. We'll tell you which setups to avoid.

Wi-Fi filtering, on request.

Your campus, hostel or office network can point at a dedicated Zaksha instance, so every device on that Wi-Fi gets the same rules, phones included. It is one policy for the whole network, and a VPN or mobile data gets around it.

Running public Wi-Fi at a café, a coworking space or a hostel? Talk to us and we'll work it out with you.
