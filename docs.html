How Zaksha works

Every time a device opens a website or an app connects to a server, it first looks up the server's name, for example youtube.com. Zaksha answers those lookups. If a name belongs to a category you've blocked, or is on your own block list, the device gets no address and the site or app can't load. Everything else works as normal.

Zaksha runs on the device itself, as an app on phones, a small agent on Windows and a profile on Mac. It keeps filtering on mobile data and on any Wi-Fi, not only at home.

Because Zaksha works on names, it sees which sites a device looked up and nothing more. It doesn't see messages, passwords, photos or what's on a page.

Setting up a device

Start in your dashboard at app.zaksha.com: choose Add device and pick the device type. Step-by-step instructions with screenshots are on Get the app. In short:

iPhone and iPad

Install the Zaksha app from the link or QR code your dashboard shows, then enter the 8-digit pairing code. The app then walks you through turning filtering on in the phone's Settings. Set a Screen Time passcode so the app can't be deleted, and keep your Apple ID password private, since Apple lets it reset that passcode.

Android

Install the Zaksha app and follow the steps in your dashboard. The standard setup alerts you if the app is removed. The fresh-phone setup is done on a factory-reset phone, and Zaksha can't be uninstalled from it.

Windows

Your dashboard gives you an installer for that PC. Run it from a parent or administrator account and accept the one Windows prompt; the PC connects by itself within a minute. Give your child a Standard Windows account, not an administrator one: anyone with the administrator password can remove Zaksha.

Filtering on Windows needs Windows 11. A Windows 10 PC can be added and shows up in your dashboard, but Zaksha doesn't filter it.

Mac

Download the configuration profile from your dashboard, open it, and approve it in System Settings → General → Device Management. As on Windows, your child should use a standard account, because an administrator can delete the profile.

Device status

Each device in your dashboard shows one of these:

OnlineThe device has been filtered by Zaksha in the last 10 minutes.
OfflineThe device hasn't been seen for a while, usually because it's switched off or asleep. Also shown when you've turned filtering off for it.
Setup pendingThe device was added but hasn't connected yet. Finish the steps on the device.
InactiveNothing from the device for more than 3 days. Check it's still in use and the app is installed.
Not filteringThe app on the device reports that filtering was switched off. You get an alert when this happens.
Apps pausediPhone only, with tamper lock on. Filtering was switched off, so the apps you chose are paused until it's back on. This is tamper lock working as intended.
Removing…You removed the device and it's taking itself off Zaksha.

Alerts

Zaksha alerts you when filtering is switched off on a device, when a device goes quiet for a long time, and before your subscription ends. Alerts arrive as a push notification on the phone where you use the dashboard. If you've verified your WhatsApp number, you also get a WhatsApp message about 10 minutes later if the problem is still there and you haven't opened the dashboard.

Rules and schedules

Categories (adult content, gambling, social media, gaming, streaming, AI chatbots and others) are switched on or off per device. You can add up to 5 sites of your own to the block list.

Schedules turn the internet off for a device at set times, such as bedtime or homework hours. You can create up to 5 schedules.

Changes reach the device within about 5 minutes; some apps hold on to an earlier answer for up to 10 minutes.

When something's wrong

A blocked site still opens

  1. Give it 10 minutes. A site that was open before you blocked it can keep working until the device looks it up again.
  2. Check the device is Online in your dashboard. If it says Not filtering, Offline or Setup pending, Zaksha isn't filtering it right now.
  3. Check the browser's own secure DNS. Chrome, Edge and Firefox can look up names themselves instead of asking the device, which skips Zaksha. On a Windows PC, turn it off: in Chrome or Edge, Settings → Privacy and security → Use secure DNS → off; in Firefox, Settings → Privacy & Security → DNS over HTTPS → Off.
  4. Check for a VPN or proxy app on the device. Android allows one VPN app at a time, and Zaksha's app uses that slot. If another VPN app takes it, the Zaksha app shows Not protected — tap to fix.
  5. Check the Windows or Mac account type. If your child's account is an administrator, they can change these settings or remove Zaksha.
  6. Windows 10 isn't filtered (see setup).

The internet stopped working

When a device can't reach Zaksha, it stops loading sites rather than going around the filter. That is deliberate, but it means a network problem can look like Zaksha "breaking the internet". On a Windows PC, these are the known cases:

  • a Wi-Fi login page on hotel, airport, train or flight Wi-Fi (see the next section);
  • connecting a VPN, such as a work VPN;
  • a network with no IPv4 at all (IPv6 only), which is rare in India.

If it's none of these, check whether other devices on the same Wi-Fi are online. If they are, contact us with the details below.

Hotel or flight Wi-Fi with a login page

Many hotels, airports and airlines make you open a login page before the Wi-Fi works. Until you log in, the network blocks every server except its own, Zaksha included. A Windows PC with Zaksha then can't find the login page either, so it can't get online.

Today, the way around this is to take Zaksha off the PC before or during the trip:

  • Before you travel, remove the device in your dashboard while you still have internet. The PC takes Zaksha off by itself within a minute.
  • If you're already stuck, sign in to the PC as an administrator and uninstall Zaksha Agent from Settings → Apps. The PC's normal settings come back; restart it if websites still don't load.

Add the PC again from your dashboard when you're back on a normal network.

Removing Zaksha

Remove the device from your dashboard first, so it stops counting towards your devices and you don't get alerts about it. Then, on the device:

  • Windows: nothing else to do. The PC uninstalls Zaksha and puts its own settings back at its next check-in, usually within a minute. If the PC is switched off, choose Remove now in the dashboard and uninstall Zaksha Agent from Settings → Apps later.
  • iPhone and iPad: after removing it in the dashboard, open the Zaksha app once, then delete it. Then open Settings → General → VPN, DNS & Device Management → DNS and delete Zaksha Filtering if it's still there, and remove Zaksha's access in Settings → Screen Time.
  • Android: uninstall the Zaksha app. A fresh-phone setup is removed by factory-resetting the phone.
  • Mac: delete the Zaksha profile in System Settings → General → Device Management.

What we keep

Zaksha keeps the names each device looked up, and whether they were blocked, for 7 days so you can see them in your dashboard. It never sees messages, passwords, photos or page content. Everything is processed and stored on servers in India. The full detail is in our Privacy Policy.

Contacting support

Email support@zaksha.com. These help us answer in one reply:

  • the device name as it appears in your dashboard, and its status;
  • what happened, and roughly when;
  • for a Windows PC, the output of this command, run in Command Prompt:
    "C:\Program Files\Zaksha\zaksha-agent.exe" status
